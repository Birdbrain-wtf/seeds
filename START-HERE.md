# Seeds, in plain words

**Seeds is the root system. The organisations people build are what grows from it.**

## What it does

Seeds keeps one record for a group, and that record holds two things:

1. **Who is here.** You join by turning up. Your group already meets, and you sign in to the meeting. Keep turning up alongside the same people and you become a member. Nobody fills in a form, and nobody issues you anything. The people who were there only step in if the record is wrong.
2. **What held up.** When a member puts something forward, it is recorded against them. If nobody objects in time, it stands. New units are created only when a contribution stands, so everything a member holds traces back to something they added.

It keeps recording both for as long as the group meets. Members get one vote each and run the machines that keep the record. There is no administrator, no fee and no treasury, and no names are held.

## What grows from it

Once a group is meeting and recording, it can build on top:

- shared projects, and rewards for the tasks inside them
- a map of what the group knows, built from what its members put forward
- funding, licences on the group's work, and charges for answers drawn from it

Each of these asks the record the same two questions: *who is a member?* and *which contributions stood?* None of them can change the answers. A group can fund, reorganise or wind up anything it has built without touching the roots, and the roots stay where anyone can check them.

## Why it matters now

Online, a person is either an account someone issued or a key they hold. An account can be closed by whoever issued it. A key says only that someone holds it, and software can hold one as easily as a person can. Seeds records something neither of them can: that a person was there, with people who know them, and what they contributed.
